## TL;DR

OpenAI is updating everyday ChatGPT conversations. Plus and Pro users get a revised GPT-5.6 Sol with a slider for choosing how much thought goes into a reply. Free and Go users are moving to GPT-5.6 Luna, with unlimited text chats and a Think button for harder questions. The rollout happens in stages.

## What's New

The revised GPT-5.6 Sol is available to Plus and Pro users in ChatGPT. OpenAI says it gives more focused answers, avoids unnecessary formatting, and uses sources more reliably when a response depends on dates, numbers, rules, or assumptions. The new slider controls how much reasoning time the model uses.

For Free and Go accounts, GPT-5.6 Luna becomes the default during the week of August 6. Unlimited text chats and the Think button are scheduled to follow the next week, subject to abuse controls. File uploads, images, and other tools keep separate limits.

This update applies to normal ChatGPT conversations. OpenAI says the GPT-5.6 Sol version used by Work and Codex is not changing as part of this release.

## Why It Matters

The practical change is less about model names and more about control. Free users can keep a text conversation going without watching a message counter, while the Think button gives them a clear way to request extra reasoning only when a task needs it. Paid users can make the same speed-versus-depth choice with the slider.

Unlimited does not mean every feature is unlimited, and rollout labels matter. Check what your own account shows before relying on a feature for work. For factual or high-stakes questions, extra reasoning still does not replace opening the cited sources and checking the answer.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["A ChatGPT account","One non-sensitive planning question you can check yourself"]}>

1. Sign in at https://chatgpt.com/ and start a new chat. Note the model name and whether you see a Think button or a reasoning slider. If neither appears, the staged rollout has not reached your account; do not upgrade just for this test.
2. Ask a small planning question with a checkable answer, such as: "Plan three errands in the shortest sensible order. List your assumptions and keep the answer under 120 words." Do not include private addresses; use neighborhood names or fictional locations.
3. Check whether the answer states its assumptions, follows the length limit, and gives a usable order. Correct any missing fact before continuing.
4. Run the same prompt with Think enabled, or move the paid reasoning slider one step higher. If your account has neither control, ask: "Review that plan for one missed constraint and revise only if needed."
5. Compare the two answers. Keep the faster mode for routine questions; use more reasoning only when the revision catches a real issue or improves the plan.
6. For any answer involving current facts, money, health, law, or safety, open the cited sources yourself. Stop if the answer has no source you can verify.
