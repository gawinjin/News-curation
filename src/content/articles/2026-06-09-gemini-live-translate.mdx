## TL;DR

Google introduced Gemini 3.5 Live Translate, a near-real-time speech-to-speech translation model for more than 70 languages. The model is rolling into Google AI Studio, Google Translate, and a private preview for Google Meet, with a focus on preserving tone, pacing, and speaker feel.

## What's New

Gemini 3.5 Live Translate is built for spoken translation, not just text conversion. Google says it detects more than 70 languages and generates natural translated speech while preserving details such as intonation, pacing, and pitch.

The rollout spans several surfaces. Developers can test through Google AI Studio and the Gemini Live API. Everyday users should watch Google Translate first, while Google Meet support is in private preview.

The best early use cases are low-risk conversations where imperfect translation is acceptable: travel phrases, language practice, and quick comprehension checks. Medical, legal, or business-critical conversations still need human review.

## Why It Matters

Live voice translation is one of the clearest ways AI becomes useful to normal people. If it works well enough, it can lower friction in travel, customer support, classrooms, and multilingual meetings.

But users should test more than literal accuracy. A translation that keeps the wrong tone can still cause confusion. Listen for whether the output sounds polite, urgent, casual, or formal in the way you intended.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["Access to Google Translate or Google AI Studio", "A short non-sensitive phrase to test"]}>

1. Open Google Translate or Google AI Studio and check whether Gemini 3.5 Live Translate is available in your account.
2. Choose a short phrase that includes tone, such as a polite request or travel question.
3. Translate it into a language you know or can verify with a fluent speaker.
4. Listen for both meaning and tone, not only individual words.
5. Avoid using live translation for medical, legal, or financial decisions unless a qualified human can review it.
