## TL;DR

OpenAI opened ChatGPT's self-serve Ads Manager to all US businesses on May 27, scrapping a $200,000 minimum that had kept it to big brands. It added cost-per-click bidding, a conversions API, and pixel-based tracking. For users, that means more advertisers — and more ads — across ChatGPT, with an international rollout already underway.

## What's New

Until now, buying ads in ChatGPT meant a $200,000 minimum commitment, so the early advertisers were names like Adobe, Ford, and Target. As of May 27, OpenAI removed that floor and let any US business open the Ads Manager itself — OpenAI's examples reach down to local car washes and dry cleaners.

The platform also grew more capable on the advertiser's side. It now supports cost-per-click bidding alongside the older cost-per-impression model, plus a Conversions API and a tracking pixel so businesses can measure what happens after a click — purchases, leads, sign-ups. OpenAI says the same tools are expanding internationally, with the UK, Mexico, Brazil, Japan, and South Korea named next.

## Why It Matters

Lowering the price of entry from $200,000 to zero is how an ad product goes from a pilot to a fixture. Expect the volume and variety of ads inside ChatGPT to climb quickly, and expect them to get better targeted — conversion tracking exists so advertisers can chase the users most likely to buy.

That is the trade-off worth naming: a free assistant funded by ads has an incentive to learn what sells you, and a measurement pixel is built to close that loop. None of this is unique to OpenAI — it is the same model that funds search and social — but it is new for a tool many people now treat as a neutral answer engine. The practical move is to know which of your data feeds personalization, and to turn down what you are not comfortable sharing.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["A ChatGPT account", "A few minutes to review your data and personalization settings"]}>

1. Learn to spot the ads: sponsored answers and product placements are labeled. Read the label before you act on a recommendation, the same way you would with a search ad.
2. Open ChatGPT Settings → Data controls and check whether "Improve the model for everyone" (chat history used for training) is on; switch it off if you would rather your conversations not be used.
3. In Settings, review any personalization or memory options and clear stored memories you do not want shaping what you are shown.
4. Treat sponsored product suggestions as leads, not verdicts — verify price and claims on the seller's own site before buying.
5. For anything sensitive (health, finances, legal), assume an ad-supported free tier is the wrong place; use a paid tier or a tool you trust with that data.

</PracticalGuide>
