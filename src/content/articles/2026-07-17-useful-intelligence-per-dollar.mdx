## TL;DR

OpenAI argues that AI value should be measured by useful work completed per dollar, not token price, purchased seats, or activity alone. Its scorecard asks whether important work gets done, what a successful result truly costs, how reliable it is, and whether value improves as use grows.

## What's New

The proposed scorecard starts with outcomes. A support team might count resolved cases, while a finance team might count accurate forecasts prepared on time. The measurement should live where the work is completed, not in a separate dashboard of prompts and tokens.

OpenAI also argues that the full cost of success matters. A low-priced model can be expensive if it needs repeated prompts and heavy review; a higher-priced model can be economical if it produces a correct result in one pass.

Reliability and scale complete the framework. Teams should track how often outputs pass review and whether each additional dollar produces proportionally more useful work as adoption expands.

## Why It Matters

This is a practical correction to vague AI productivity claims. High usage can mean real value, or it can mean people are retrying bad outputs. Counting finished work and correction time makes that difference visible.

The framework is also useful for individuals. You do not need a finance department to measure whether an AI tool saves time. A few repeated trials can show whether it improves a real workflow or only makes the first draft feel faster.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="20 min" prerequisites={["One repeated task that you already use AI for","A timer and a simple note or spreadsheet"]}>

1. Choose one repeated task, such as summarizing meeting notes into assigned actions.
2. Define "done" before starting: every action has an owner, deadline, and source note, with no invented details.
3. Run the task three times with similar inputs and record total time, tool cost if visible, retries, and correction time.
4. Count only outputs that meet your definition of done without unresolved errors.
5. Divide the total cost and time by successful outputs, then compare that result with your previous manual process or another model.

</PracticalGuide>
