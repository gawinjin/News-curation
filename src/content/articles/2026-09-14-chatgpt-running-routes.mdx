## TL;DR

ChatGPT Work can turn a requested running distance into a mapped loop and downloadable route files. Treat that output as a draft: begin at a public landmark rather than your home, then check every road, crossing, and trail against current maps and official closure information before you run it.

## What's New

Simon Willison asked ChatGPT Work, with GPT-6 Astra using Max reasoning, to make 5 km and 10 km loops from a chosen starting point. After 27 minutes, it returned an interactive route view along with GPX and GeoJSON files that could be downloaded.

ChatGPT reported that it found the location through Nominatim, collected nearby OpenStreetMap roads and trails with Overpass, and calculated the routes in its cloud environment. The example 5 km option measured 5.1 km and combined neighborhood streets with a coastal trail.

The finished map used HTML, D3, and route geometry stored as JSON. Willison could not inspect the program or its exact execution inside ChatGPT, however, and a later request for the Python code did not recover it after the conversation had apparently been compacted.

## Why It Matters

This is a useful starting point when you want a loop of a specific length without plotting every turn yourself. The map and standard route files make the result easier to review and move into a navigation or fitness app you already use.

But a plausible line on a map is not proof that a route is open, public, continuous, or safe today. OpenStreetMap data comes without a warranty, and the generated process is not fully inspectable. Protect your location by starting from a public landmark, reject uncertain segments, and let current official information and conditions on the ground overrule the AI.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="20-30 min" prerequisites={["ChatGPT Work access at chatgpt.com or in the ChatGPT desktop app","A public landmark or intersection instead of a home address","OpenStreetMap and local official road, trail, or park-closure information"]}>

1. Open chatgpt.com and start a Work task. Select GPT-6 Astra only if it appears in the model picker; stop this exact test if Work or Astra is unavailable.
2. Ask for separate 5 km and 10 km loops starting and ending at a public landmark, using current OpenStreetMap data. Request sidewalks or multi-use paths, no private roads or highways, named roads and crossings, uncertainty flags, maps, GPX, and GeoJSON. Do not provide your home address.
3. Let the task run for up to 30 minutes. Confirm the result includes two stated distances, embedded maps, and downloadable GPX or GeoJSON files; request any missing output before continuing.
4. Check every named road, trail, crossing, and loop connection on OpenStreetMap and your local authority's closure pages. Reject any private, closed, pedestrian-unsafe, or uncertain segment.
5. Save and preview the GPX or GeoJSON in a navigation or fitness app you already trust. Obey signs and traffic controls, share your plan, and turn back if real conditions differ from the generated route.
