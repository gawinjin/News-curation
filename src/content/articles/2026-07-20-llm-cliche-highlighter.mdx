## TL;DR

Simon Willison's free browser tool highlights formulaic phrases often found in AI-assisted writing. Paste a draft or load a public URL, then review matches in context and switch individual pattern checks on or off. Treat the results as editing suggestions, not evidence that AI wrote the text.

## What's New

Willison released a browser-based analyzer for recurring phrases he noticed across AI-assisted writing. It began with ten patterns and marks matching language inside the surrounding sentence, making each result easier to judge in context.

You can paste text directly or ask the tool to load a public URL. Its pattern detectors are individually adjustable, while a match list helps you move through the flagged passages.

The accompanying development log links the source code and records additions such as local storage, URL loading, and extra pattern checks.

## Why It Matters

Formulaic wording can make an otherwise useful email, article, or presentation sound generic. This tool gives writers a quick second editing pass by pointing to phrases worth reconsidering rather than rewriting the draft for them.

A match does not prove that AI produced the text: people use the same constructions too. The useful question is whether a highlighted phrase serves the reader or can be replaced with something more precise.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="5 min" prerequisites={["A modern web browser","A paragraph to check, or a public article URL"]}>

1. Open the highlighter and paste text into the main box; analysis runs as you type.
2. Select 'Load example' and confirm the summary and highlighted-text sections populate.
3. Expand 'Patterns' and toggle detectors; confirm the match count or highlights change.
4. Hover a highlight to identify its rule, then review every flagged phrase in the Matches list.
5. Optionally enter a public article URL and select 'Load URL'; avoid private or paywalled material because loading uses a third-party text fetcher.

</PracticalGuide>
