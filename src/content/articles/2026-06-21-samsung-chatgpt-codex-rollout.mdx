## TL;DR

OpenAI says Samsung Electronics is rolling out ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex to a broad employee base, including all Samsung Electronics employees in Korea and Device eXperience employees worldwide. The important signal is that AI is becoming managed workplace infrastructure, not just a personal productivity shortcut.

## What's New

OpenAI describes the Samsung rollout as one of its largest enterprise deployments to date. Under the agreement, ChatGPT Enterprise and Codex will be available to Samsung Electronics employees in Korea and to Device eXperience employees globally.

Samsung plans to use the tools across software development, marketing, product development, manufacturing, corporate functions, and other areas. OpenAI also says Codex is now used weekly by more than 5 million people across technical and non-technical workflows.

The announcement is part of a broader Korea push for OpenAI, including education, consumer messaging, and enterprise adoption partnerships.

## Why It Matters

Large companies are not just buying chatbots. They are deciding how AI access, data boundaries, app approvals, audit logs, and employee training should work at scale.

For everyone else, Samsung is a useful prompt: if your team already uses AI informally, the next step is not more experimentation. It is deciding which workflows are allowed, who reviews outputs, and what data is off limits.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A workplace AI pilot, team tool rollout, or interest in evaluating one"]}>

1. Write down one workflow where your team already uses AI informally, such as drafting, analysis, code review, or reporting.
2. Check whether the tool is personal, team-managed, or enterprise-managed.
3. Ask who controls data retention, user access, app approvals, and audit logs.
4. Define one allowed use, one banned use, and one review checkpoint before expanding access.
5. Only scale the rollout after a real task shows measurable time saved or quality improved.
