## TL;DR

OpenAI published research arguing that agents are shifting work from short chatbot interactions to delegated, long-horizon tasks. The useful habit is to stop asking only quick questions and start testing agents on bounded work that takes 30 to 90 minutes and can be reviewed.

## What's New

OpenAI says Codex adoption moved from engineers into departments such as Legal, Finance, Recruiting, Marketing, Operations, and Customer Support. The paper argues that agents let workers cross task boundaries, especially when technical execution used to require specialized help.

The paper reports that many users now ask Codex for tasks estimated to take more than 30 minutes or more than one hour of human work. OpenAI also says heavy internal users run many hours of agent work in parallel during a single day.

OpenAI frames this as an economic shift: the unit of AI work is becoming a delegated task, not a single answer.

## Why It Matters

If agents are only used like chatbots, most people miss the change. The value is not a clever answer; it is sustained work with tools, files, context, and a reviewable output.

The risk is delegating the wrong work. Good agent tasks are bounded, reversible, and easier to inspect than to perform manually. Bad ones require judgment you cannot check or access you should not grant.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["Access to a coding or work agent","One task you can safely delegate"]}>

1. Pick one task that would take you 30 to 90 minutes, not a five-second question.
2. Write the desired outcome, inputs, constraints, and what the agent should not change.
3. Ask the agent to produce a plan before doing the work.
4. Approve only the first small step and inspect the result.
5. Scale up only if the task is reviewable, reversible, and easier to check than to do from scratch.

</PracticalGuide>
