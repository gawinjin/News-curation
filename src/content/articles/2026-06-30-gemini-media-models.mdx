## TL;DR

Google released Nano Banana 2 Lite for fast image generation and Gemini Omni Flash for video generation and conversational editing. Both are available to developers, while Nano Banana 2 Lite is also rolling out across Google consumer surfaces.

## What's New

Nano Banana 2 Lite is the speed-and-cost model in Google's image family. Google says it can produce text-to-image outputs in about four seconds and is priced for high-volume drafting.

Gemini Omni Flash is now available to developers through Google AI Studio, the Gemini API, and the enterprise platform. It supports video generation and conversational editing from text, image, and video inputs.

Google says Nano Banana 2 Lite is also rolling out to products such as AI Mode in Search, the Gemini app, NotebookLM, Google Photos, Stitch, Flow, and Ads.

## Why It Matters

Fast media models change the workflow from "make one asset and wait" to "draft, compare, and revise quickly." That is useful for teachers, marketers, creators, and product teams even if they never touch the API.

The same caution still applies: generated media can be wrong, awkward, or too close to a real person or brand. Treat it as a draft until a human reviews it.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A Google account","Google AI Studio access"]}>

1. Open Google AI Studio and look for Nano Banana 2 Lite or Gemini image/video model options.
2. Start with a harmless image prompt, such as a product moodboard or classroom poster draft.
3. Check whether the output has readable text, consistent characters, and no unexpected brand or person likeness.
4. If Omni Flash is available in your account, try one short video-editing request using non-private footage.
5. Save the prompt and result so you can compare quality, latency, and cost before using it in a repeated workflow.
