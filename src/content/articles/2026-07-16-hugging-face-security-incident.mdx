## TL;DR

Hugging Face disclosed an intrusion that began in its dataset-processing pipeline and spread to internal systems. It found no evidence that public models, datasets, Spaces, packages, or container images were altered, but its customer-data review continues. The company recommends that users rotate access tokens and inspect recent activity.

## What's New

Hugging Face says a malicious dataset exploited two code-execution paths, allowing an attacker to reach processing workers, obtain credentials, and move into several clusters. The campaign used an autonomous agent framework to perform thousands of actions across short-lived environments.

The company closed the vulnerable paths, rebuilt affected nodes, rotated service credentials, tightened admission controls, and brought in external forensic specialists. It says a limited set of internal datasets and service credentials were accessed, while the assessment of possible partner or customer impact is still underway.

Hugging Face used AI-assisted detection and analyzed more than 17,000 logged events with a self-hosted open model. For ordinary account holders, the immediate action is simpler: replace access tokens and remove permissions that are no longer needed.

## Why It Matters

Access tokens can let notebooks, apps, and automation read private repositories or publish changes. A long-lived token with broad write permission increases the damage possible if it is exposed.

Rotating tokens is useful only if old credentials are revoked and every legitimate app receives the replacement. This is also a good time to switch to one fine-grained token per app, so a future rotation does not disrupt everything at once.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min, plus app updates" prerequisites={["A Hugging Face account","Access to the apps or services that use your Hugging Face tokens"]}>

1. Sign in to Hugging Face and open Settings, then Access Tokens.
2. Identify each token by the app, notebook, or service that uses it; delete tokens you cannot account for.
3. Refresh the remaining tokens and prefer a separate fine-grained token with only the required repositories and permissions for each app.
4. Replace the old value in every legitimate app or secret store, restart that service, and confirm it still connects.
5. Enable two-factor authentication, review recent repository changes, and contact Hugging Face security if you find activity you do not recognize.
