## TL;DR

Meta released Muse Spark 1.1 with stronger coding and long-task abilities. It now powers Thinking mode in Meta AI, while a new Model API gives developers their first broad route to use Meta's current flagship model in other products.

## What's New

Muse Spark 1.1 is an update to the model Meta introduced in April. Meta says the new version is better at coding and agent-style work that requires planning, tools, and longer sequences of actions.

The model is available to ordinary users through Thinking mode in the Meta AI app and website. Meta has also opened a developer version through its Model API, turning Muse Spark from an assistant confined mostly to Meta's products into a model other services can build around.

This is a proprietary release, not a downloadable open-weight model. Availability can also vary by region and account, so the practical test is whether Thinking mode appears for you today.

## Why It Matters

Meta already places its assistant inside products used by billions of people. A stronger reasoning model can therefore reach everyday chats, planning, and creative work quickly, without requiring users to install another app.

The useful question is not whether Meta's benchmarks beat another lab's chart. It is whether Thinking mode gives a more complete, checkable answer on a task you actually do. A side-by-side test with the normal mode will tell you more than the launch claims.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["A Meta AI account","Access to meta.ai in your region"]}>

1. Open [Meta AI](https://www.meta.ai/) and sign in.
2. Look for Thinking mode in the model or response controls. If it is missing, your account or region may not have the rollout yet.
3. Give it a bounded task such as planning a three-day trip under a fixed budget, including opening hours and travel time.
4. Ask it to list which details came from current web results and which are assumptions.
