## TL;DR

OpenAI has launched ChatGPT for Teens for users aged 13 to 17. It keeps ordinary learning and creative tools while adding stronger safety defaults, homework reminders, Study Hours, break prompts, and family controls. Parents can manage selected settings, but they cannot read a teen's conversations.

## What's New

ChatGPT now places people it identifies as 13 to 17 into a dedicated teen experience. The product emphasizes step-by-step learning through Study Mode, quizzes, visual explanations, and reminders that redirect apparent homework shortcuts toward working through the problem. Study Hours can make that learning mode the default during a chosen schedule.

Teen accounts also receive stronger protections around self-harm, violence, eating disorders, dangerous activities, explicit content, and relationship-like behavior. Break reminders and sensitive-image warnings are intended to reduce unhealthy use and accidental sharing. These systems can still make mistakes and do not replace a trusted adult, teacher, counselor, or emergency service.

When a teen and guardian link their separate accounts, the guardian can manage quiet hours, Study Mode, memory, voice, images, model training, and some ChatGPT Work permissions. Linking does not reveal chat history or live activity. Either person can unlink the accounts, and the guardian is notified if the teen does so.

## Why It Matters

Many families already use general-purpose AI for homework, explanations, and personal questions. A separate teen experience makes the defaults more visible, but the useful part is the conversation it enables: which tasks AI may help with, what should remain private, when answers need checking, and when a person should take over.

The distinction between Study Hours and Quiet Hours is important. Study Hours changes how eligible new chats begin; it does not block ChatGPT. Quiet Hours restricts access during selected times. Families should test both settings with harmless material and keep school rules and human support above the tool's suggestions.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["Separate ChatGPT accounts for a teen and parent or guardian","Access to the invitation email or phone number"]}>

1. Sign in to the teen and guardian accounts separately. Do not share passwords or use one account for both people.
2. In either account, open **Settings > Parental controls**. The guardian can select **Add family member**; the teen can select **Invite a parent**. Send the invitation by email or phone and accept it from the other account.
3. From the guardian account, open the teen under **Family members**. Review each control together, including model training, memory, voice, images, Study Mode, Study Hours, and Quiet Hours. Leave protections on unless there is a clear reason to change them.
4. Turn on Study Mode or schedule a short Study Hours window. Remember that Study Hours changes the default mode, while Quiet Hours is the setting that blocks access.
5. In the teen account, start a new chat about a harmless topic they already understand. Ask for an explanation, two practice questions, and feedback on their answers. Check whether the tool guides the reasoning instead of simply completing graded work.
6. End by agreeing on three rules: follow the school's AI policy, verify important answers elsewhere, and move sensitive health, safety, or relationship concerns to a trusted person rather than relying on ChatGPT.
