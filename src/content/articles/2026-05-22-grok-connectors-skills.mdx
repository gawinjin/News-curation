## TL;DR

On May 22 xAI added a second wave of Grok connectors — Vercel, Canva, Gamma, and S&P Global — that work without leaving the chat, on web, iOS, and Android. Days earlier it shipped Skills: saved instructions for tone, format, and recurring tasks that carry across every future conversation. Together they push Grok from a chatbot toward a place you get work done.

## What's New

Connectors let Grok reach into other services and act on your behalf. The new batch covers building and deploying a website (Vercel), designing graphics (Canva), making slide decks (Gamma), and pulling live market data (S&P Global). They join earlier connectors for Gmail, Google Drive, GitHub, and Notion, and run inside the same chat window on web and mobile.

Skills, which landed just before on May 18, solve the other half of the problem: repeating yourself. Instead of re-explaining your preferred tone, output format, or a recurring workflow each session, you define it once as a Skill and Grok applies it across future chats. The combination — persistent instructions plus tools that act on real accounts — is what turns a single answer into a repeatable task.

## Why It Matters

This is the same direction every major assistant is moving: stop answering questions, start doing the job. Grok's angle is distribution — it is bundled with X Premium, so a large existing subscriber base gets a build-a-site or make-a-deck tool without paying for another app.

Be clear-eyed about two things. First, connectors mean granting Grok access to accounts that matter — your email, your code, your designs — so the convenience comes with the same access risk as any agent. Second, "make a website" or "build a deck" in one prompt is impressive in a demo and uneven in practice; expect to review and fix what it produces rather than ship it untouched. Connect the low-stakes tools first, and treat the output as a draft.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A Grok account (X Premium for full access)", "The apps or accounts you want to connect"]}>

1. Open Grok on web or mobile and find the connectors menu. Start by linking one low-risk service — Notion or Google Drive — rather than email or code on the first try.
2. Run a small real task through it, like "summarize the docs in this Drive folder," and check that Grok read the right files.
3. Create your first Skill: write down your default tone and output format (for example, "concise, bullet points, no preamble") and save it, then open a fresh chat to confirm it applies automatically.
4. Try one connector-powered build — a one-page site in Vercel or a short deck in Gamma — and treat the result as a first draft to edit, not a finished product.
5. Review what you have granted: open the connectors list periodically and disconnect anything you are not actively using, especially accounts with sensitive data.
