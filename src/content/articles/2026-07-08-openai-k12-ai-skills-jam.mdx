## TL;DR

OpenAI Academy is running AI Skills Jam sessions for K-12 educators this summer. The practical signal is simple: schools are moving from abstract AI policy talk toward hands-on training for lesson planning, communication, and admin work.

## What's New

OpenAI Academy and the Walton Family Foundation are hosting in-person AI Skills Jam sessions for K-12 teachers, administrators, and district leaders. The announcement lists city dates running from July through early September.

OpenAI says the workshops will focus on everyday school tasks: lesson planning, parent and staff communication, administrative work, and responsible AI adoption. The company also points participants to OpenAI Academy for follow-up resources.

The announcement cites research from the Walton Family Foundation and Gallup: teachers who use AI weekly estimate saving 5.9 hours per week on average. Treat that as self-reported, not guaranteed, but it explains why schools are paying attention.

## Why It Matters

Teachers do not need another vague AI keynote. They need safe, repeatable habits that save time without leaking student data or turning classroom judgment over to a chatbot.

For parents and school leaders, the useful question is no longer whether AI will enter schools. It already has. The better question is whether teachers get practical training, privacy rules, and time to test the tools before students rely on them.

1. Check the OpenAI announcement for a nearby Skills Jam date or a district partner you recognize.

3. Pick one low-risk task, such as drafting a parent email or turning existing notes into a lesson outline.
