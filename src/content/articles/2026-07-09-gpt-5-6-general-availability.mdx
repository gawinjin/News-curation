## TL;DR

GPT-5.6 has moved from limited previews to general availability. The family has three versions: Sol for the hardest work, Terra for a balance of capability and speed, and Luna for high-volume lower-cost tasks. Availability still varies by ChatGPT plan and product.

## What's New

OpenAI now presents GPT-5.6 as a family rather than one model. Sol is the flagship for complex professional work, Terra is the general-purpose middle option, and Luna prioritizes speed and cost for repeated tasks.

The release follows the earlier Sol preview and makes the family available through ChatGPT and the API. The same models are also appearing in partner products, but plan limits, administrator settings, and staged rollouts can affect which names are visible.

The model label alone does not tell you which version is best for your work. A cheaper or faster model can be the better choice when the task is easy to check, while a difficult analysis may justify the additional time or cost of Sol.

## Why It Matters

Users now have to make a practical tradeoff instead of automatically selecting the largest model. The right comparison is not benchmark position; it is whether a model produces a usable result with fewer retries and less human correction.

A small side-by-side test is more reliable than assuming the flagship is always worth it. Use the same prompt and source material, then compare factual accuracy, instruction following, and how much editing each answer needs.

1. Pick a real but low-risk task, such as summarizing a page you know well into five decisions and three open questions.
2. Run the same prompt and source text with two GPT-5.6 versions visible in your model picker.
3. Do not reveal one model's output to the other; keep the conditions identical.
4. Score each answer for factual errors, missed instructions, useful detail, and editing time.
5. Use the faster or cheaper option as your default when quality is close, and reserve the stronger model for tasks where it clearly reduces correction work.
