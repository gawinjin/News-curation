## TL;DR

Microsoft introduced seven new MAI models covering reasoning, coding, image creation, transcription, and voice. The launch matters because Microsoft is building a coordinated model family instead of relying only on outside labs. Its public MAI Playground gives users a simple place to see which models are available and compare their outputs.

## What's New

The new family includes MAI-Thinking-1 for reasoning, MAI-Code-1-Flash for coding, MAI-Image-2.5 and its Flash variant, MAI-Transcribe-1.5, and MAI-Voice-2 with a Flash version planned. Microsoft says it trained the models in-house and designed them to work across its products.

Microsoft is also opening broader distribution through Foundry and developer platforms including OpenRouter, Fireworks, and Baseten. Some models are already visible in the MAI Playground, which separates text, image, voice, and transcription tasks.

Performance comparisons in the announcement are Microsoft-reported. The useful reader test is not whether one model wins every benchmark, but whether a specialized model produces a better result for a specific task than a general chatbot.

## Why It Matters

AI products are becoming collections of specialized models. The model transcribing a meeting may be different from the one creating an image or reasoning through a plan, even when they appear inside one product.

That gives users a better way to evaluate AI: compare models on the same small task and judge accuracy, speed, and usefulness. A model name matters less than whether the output works for the job in front of you.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["A web browser", "A Microsoft account may be required to generate results"]}>

1. Open https://playground.microsoft.ai/ and review the model categories shown in the sidebar.
2. Select an available text or image model and read any usage notice before signing in or submitting content.
3. Enter a small, non-sensitive task with a clear success condition, such as summarizing a short paragraph into three bullets.
4. Run the same task with another available model or mode.
5. Compare accuracy, response time, and whether either result needs correction before deciding which model fits the task.

</PracticalGuide>
