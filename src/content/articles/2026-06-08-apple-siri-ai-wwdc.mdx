## TL;DR

Apple used WWDC26 to preview Siri AI, a rebuilt assistant tied to the next generation of Apple Intelligence. It can use personal context, answer questions about what is on screen, take actions across apps, and keep conversations in a dedicated Siri app. The catch: the rollout is beta, device-limited, language-limited, and region-limited.

## What's New

Apple says Siri AI is deeply integrated across iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Apple Vision Pro, and CarPlay. The assistant can search across messages, emails, photos, and notes, answer questions about screen content, use web information, and perform more app actions.

The company also introduced related Apple Intelligence features: Safari Notify Me for page changes, Messages suggestions that can create reminders or notes, improved Dictation, Visual Intelligence on more products, and a dedicated Siri app that syncs conversation history with iCloud.

Availability is the practical constraint. Developer testing starts now for supported platforms, public beta arrives later, and free software updates are planned for fall. Siri AI starts in English later this year, will not initially be available on iPhone and iPad in the EU, and will not be available in China while Apple works through regulatory requirements.

## Why It Matters

Apple's AI story matters because it sits inside devices people already use for messages, photos, calendars, calls, and payments. A more capable Siri could become useful precisely because it can see more personal context than a standalone chatbot.

That also means users should check device eligibility, language support, and privacy settings before assuming the feature is ready for them. The best first step is not installing every beta; it is confirming whether your device and region can actually use the new assistant.
