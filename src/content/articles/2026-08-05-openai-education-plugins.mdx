## TL;DR

OpenAI has released three education plugins for K-12 teachers, college educators, and college students. They are available only through managed ChatGPT Edu or district ChatGPT for Teachers deployments, where institutions decide which tools and permissions are allowed; an individual consumer account is not enough.

## What's New

Each plugin combines instructions, skills, connected apps, and common workflows for a particular education role. The K-12 version can help teachers make differentiated resources, interactive visuals, and standards-aligned materials while leaving pedagogy, grading, and agent actions under educator control.

The college educator version can use approved course materials, calendars, documents, and tools to prepare syllabi, assessments, teaching sites, and content for a learning-management system. The student version can turn selected course sources into tutoring sessions, quizzes, flashcards, study guides, and interactive explanations.

This is education-specific tooling, not a generic plugin release or another training course. Access depends on a managed education workspace, and administrators can restrict apps, data sources, read or write actions, approvals, and synchronization boundaries.

## Why It Matters

The role-based setup can reduce the time people spend explaining context before they begin a lesson-planning or study task. A teacher can start from an approved course source; a student can ask for practice based on selected material instead of beginning with a blank chat.

Institutional control is the tradeoff as well as the safeguard. These plugins are not available to everyone, and their useful connections may be limited by school policy. Before real classroom use, people should inspect permissions, test with non-sensitive material, verify every output independently, and keep educators responsible for approval and grading.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["Access to a managed ChatGPT Edu or ChatGPT for Teachers district workspace","The matching education plugin enabled by your workspace administrator","A fictional or public learning objective with no student records"]}>

1. Open the ChatGPT plugin directory while signed into your education workspace and search for the K-12 Educator, College Educator, or College Student plugin. Stop and ask your administrator if it is absent or disabled.
2. Open the listing and review its included apps, requested permissions, terms, and privacy policy. Connect only an institution-approved app, and keep the first test read-only and limited to non-sensitive material.
3. Invoke the plugin with an at-mention or the plus menu, then give it a fictional objective such as comparing three quarters with five eighths. Ask for three exit-ticket questions, an answer key, and the source concept behind each answer.
4. Solve every question independently and check each answer and claimed standard against the original material. Revise or discard anything unsupported.
5. Keep the result private until an educator approves it. Do not publish, grade, message students, or connect private records during this first test.

</PracticalGuide>
