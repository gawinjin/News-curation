## TL;DR

Samsung previewed intelligent eyewear built with Google Gemini, a camera, and Android XR. It is not yet a product most people can buy, but its key idea is already testable: let an AI see what you see and respond in real time. Try that workflow safely with Gemini Live on an Android phone.

## What's New

Samsung says it is developing lightweight eyewear with Gentle Monster and Warby Parker. The design combines a built-in camera, Gemini, Android XR, and Qualcomm's Snapdragon AR1 Gen 1 chip. Samsung has not announced a public price or firm sale date.

The demonstrated uses include summarizing long messages, translating conversations, saving a whiteboard or meeting into Samsung Notes, giving navigation help, and sharing a live view with Gemini. Samsung also says the glasses can work with a Galaxy Watch9 for gesture controls.

Samsung claims up to nine hours of use from one charge and as many as seven additional recharges from the case. Availability, language support, connected-device requirements, and some contextual features will vary. Camera use and recording also remain subject to consent and local law.

## Why It Matters

Smart glasses move visual AI from a phone you take out occasionally to a camera that is close to your point of view. That could make translation, directions, and note capture quicker, but it also makes it easier to include people, screens, or private spaces without thinking.

The useful test today is not whether the frame looks fashionable. It is whether a camera-aware assistant helps with a simple task, whether you can tell when sharing is active, and whether you understand what may be saved. Gemini Live on Android provides that smaller, reversible preview.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["An Android phone or tablet with the Gemini app","A personal Google account","A non-sensitive object or document"]}>

1. Open Gemini, tap your profile picture, and review Gemini Apps Activity before sharing anything. Use a non-sensitive test and avoid other people, private screens, or confidential documents.
2. Start Gemini Live and tap the camera button. Point it at a book cover or household object and ask: "Describe only what is visible, then tell me what you are uncertain about."
3. Check the answer against the object yourself. Tap the camera button again and confirm that sharing has stopped.
4. Optional: open a dummy note with no personal information, share your screen in Gemini Live, and ask for a two-sentence summary. Remember that screen sharing exposes the full screen.
5. Stop sharing from Gemini or the Android notification, then lock the screen and confirm the session does not resume automatically. Ask permission before testing around another person.

</PracticalGuide>
