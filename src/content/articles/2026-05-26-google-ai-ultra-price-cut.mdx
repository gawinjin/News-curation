## TL;DR

Google AI Ultra now starts at $100 a month instead of $250, and the old $250 tier drops to $200 with the same features. The timing is deliberate: the cheaper Ultra is the gateway to Gemini Spark, Google's new background agent, which opens to US Ultra subscribers the week of May 26.

## What's New

Google restructured AI Ultra at I/O. Entry pricing falls from $250 to $100 a month; the previous $250 plan is now $200, and Google says the two tiers keep the same capabilities. Google also revised usage limits across its plans.

The cut lands alongside Gemini Spark's beta, which is limited to US Ultra subscribers starting the week of May 26. In practice, $100 is now the price of admission to Google's most agentic features — Spark, the new background agent, plus higher limits on Gemini 3.5.

## Why It Matters

A more-than-half price cut on a flagship plan is a signal about competition, not generosity. Google is racing OpenAI and Anthropic for paying users, and lowering the entry point is how you nudge people up from free tiers. For you, the real question is whether the features you would actually use justify $100 a month.

Most people do not need Ultra. The free and mid tiers already cover everyday questions, drafting, and search. Ultra earns its price only if you will lean on background agents, high usage limits, or the video tools — and even then, those agent features are brand new and worth testing before you commit to a year.

1. Write down what you actually use AI for in a typical week — search, drafting, coding, images, video — and how often you hit limits on your current plan.
2. In the Gemini app or Google One, compare your current tier against the $100 Ultra plan feature by feature, and mark which Ultra-only features you would genuinely use.
3. If Spark is the draw, remember it is US-only beta on Ultra right now; confirm it is available in your region before paying.
4. Subscribe monthly, not annually. Use it for two weeks on real tasks, then check whether you reached the paid-only features often enough to keep it.
5. If you rarely touch the Ultra-only features, drop back to a cheaper tier — the free and mid plans cover most everyday use.
