## TL;DR

The leaders of Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google DeepMind now broadly agree that the most capable AI models need independent testing and common standards before release. Their proposals differ on whether enforcement should resemble an aviation regulator, an industry-funded standards body, or an international certification system.

## What's New

Axios compared recent written proposals from Dario Amodei, Sam Altman, and Demis Hassabis and found a shared core: outside scrutiny, one coherent standards system, attention to cyber and biological risks, and rules focused on frontier models rather than every AI project.

Hassabis proposes a federally overseen public-private standards body, initially using voluntary reviews up to 30 days before release. Amodei argues for mandatory third-party testing and government power to block models that create unacceptable risks in specified areas. OpenAI favors national standards that can support a wider international framework.

The differences matter. A complex certification system may improve accountability, but it can also favor established labs that already have legal, security, and policy teams. Startups, researchers, and open-model developers could face a heavier relative burden.

## Why It Matters

Users cannot independently test every safety claim, but they can look for evidence that a provider explains what was evaluated, who performed the testing, which risks remain, and how incidents are handled after release.

This emerging agreement also gives policymakers a clearer question: not whether frontier models should be assessed, but who sets the threshold, who can stop a release, and how smaller participants remain represented.
