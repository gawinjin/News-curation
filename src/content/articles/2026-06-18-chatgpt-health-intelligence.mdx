## TL;DR

OpenAI says GPT-5.5 Instant now gives stronger health responses to free ChatGPT users. That is useful, but it does not make ChatGPT your doctor. The best everyday use is preparing better questions, spotting urgency, and turning confusing health information into notes you can verify with a professional.

## What's New

OpenAI says health is one of ChatGPT's biggest use cases, with more than 230 million people asking health and wellness questions each week. The company says GPT-5.5 Instant improves how ChatGPT recognizes urgent-care situations, asks for context, explains uncertainty, and makes complex information easier to understand.

The update is tied to physician-led evaluation work. OpenAI says a network of more than 260 physicians across 60 countries helped define what good health answers should look like, and its HealthBench evaluations test qualities like accuracy, safety, communication, completeness, and appropriate escalation.

OpenAI also says GPT-5.5 Instant is available to free ChatGPT users, subject to limits, which matters because health questions are not limited to paid subscribers.

## Why It Matters

People already bring symptoms, lab results, medication questions, insurance confusion, and appointment anxiety to ChatGPT. Better health behavior from the default model can help, especially when the answer encourages context, uncertainty, and professional follow-up.

The danger is over-trusting a fluent answer. A better health chatbot is still a chatbot. Use it to prepare, clarify, and organize, then let a qualified person handle diagnosis, treatment, and urgent decisions.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="10 min" prerequisites={["A ChatGPT account","A non-emergency health question or appointment to prepare for"]}>

1. Do not use ChatGPT for emergencies; call local emergency services or a clinician if symptoms are urgent.
2. Open ChatGPT and describe your question with age range, relevant symptoms, timing, medications, and what you already know.
3. Ask ChatGPT to list red flags that would require urgent care, then check whether any apply to you now.
4. Ask for three questions to bring to a doctor, pharmacist, insurer, or clinic.
5. Save the output as appointment notes, not as a diagnosis, and confirm decisions with a qualified professional.

</PracticalGuide>
