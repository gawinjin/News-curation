## TL;DR

Gemini Spark, Google's cloud agent that keeps working when your phone is closed, reaches US Google AI Ultra subscribers the week of May 26. It runs on Gemini 3.5 and Google Cloud virtual machines, and Google pitches it for jobs like scanning statements, triaging email, or drafting documents — with you setting the tasks and the limits.

## What's New

Spark first went to trusted testers when Google announced it at I/O on May 19. The week of May 26 it opens as a beta to Google AI Ultra subscribers in the United States. Google also repriced Ultra: the entry plan now costs $100 a month, down from $250, and the former $250 tier drops to $200, with Google saying both keep the same capabilities. Both include Spark.

Spark is not a chat window. It runs on dedicated virtual machines in Google Cloud, so it can carry out long, multi-step jobs in the background even when your laptop is shut. It is built on Gemini 3.5 and Google's Antigravity agent harness.

Google's examples are specific: comb credit-card statements for forgotten subscriptions, watch a child's school emails and turn them into a daily summary, or convert meeting notes into a Google Doc and draft the follow-up. Google says Spark acts under your direction.

## Why It Matters

The change is from answering questions to acting on your accounts. That is more useful and more consequential at the same time: an agent that reads your email and statements needs access to exactly the data people most want to guard. The price cut compounds it — halving the entry cost to $100 makes a capable agent far easier to justify, so more people will hand real accounts to a system that is still in beta.

So treat the first weeks as a trial, not a handover. Start Spark on low-stakes, read-only work, check what it actually did, and keep your highest-risk connections — banking, primary email — disconnected until it earns them.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A Google AI Ultra subscription (US)", "A Google account whose activity you can review"]}>

1. Confirm your plan: in the Gemini app or Google One, check you are on AI Ultra ($100 or $200) in the US — Spark's beta is limited to those tiers.
2. Open Gemini, find Spark in the app's agent or tasks area, and connect only one low-risk source first, such as Calendar rather than Gmail or banking.
3. Give Spark one read-only job, like summarizing your next week of calendar events, and let it run in the background.
4. Come back and check its work against the source: did it miss anything, invent anything, or act beyond what you asked?
5. Only once it is reliable on small tasks, decide whether to grant a higher-stakes connection — and write down what it may do without asking you first.
