## TL;DR

Google's Gemini 3.8 text-to-speech models can turn a script into directed narration or a two-person scene. Flash prioritises detailed creative control, while Flash-Lite is designed for cheaper, higher-volume generation. You can compare them in a browser playground using a short script and your own Gemini API key.

## What's New

Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS accepts detailed directions for pacing, acting, dialect changes, nonverbal sounds and small listener reactions. It can design a voice from a written description in more than 100 languages and dialects, or draw from Google's library of over 2,000 ready-made voices. Google says it can also keep characters consistent through longer recordings and produce a two-speaker conversation from one script.

Flash-Lite TTS targets higher-volume work at a lower cost. Both models are available through Google AI Studio and the Gemini API, and generated clips carry a SynthID watermark. Voice replication requires a 30-second sample that the user owns or has permission to use, plus a matching spoken consent recording; it is unavailable in several regions.

Simon Willison's browser playground provides a quicker way to compare single-voice and multi-speaker results. It sends requests from the browser to Google's API using the reader's own key, which the page says remains in memory rather than browser storage or shared URLs.

## Why It Matters

The useful change is control. Someone making a narrated presentation, language sample or short dialogue can describe how each line should sound instead of accepting a single default reading. The two tiers also make it practical to compare a more directed performance with a lower-cost option before choosing one for repeated work.

That convenience still comes with boundaries. Start with a built-in voice and non-confidential text, especially when testing through a third-party page. Check the live pricing and data-use terms before uploading real material, and treat voice replication as a consent-based feature rather than a shortcut for copying someone else's voice.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A Google account and a Gemini API key from Google AI Studio","A current JavaScript-enabled browser with audio playback","A short, non-confidential script you wrote or have permission to process"]}>

1. Open [Google AI Studio's API-key page](https://aistudio.google.com/apikey), sign in, and create or copy a Gemini API key. Do not expose the key in a screenshot or use it on a shared computer.
2. Open the [Gemini 3.8 TTS Playground](https://tools.simonwillison.net/gemini-tts-playground), paste the key under Connection, and wait for the voice list to load. The third-party page says the key stays in page memory and requests go directly to Google's API.
3. Keep Single voice selected, choose Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS, select a built-in voice, and enter this original script: 'The museum closes in ten minutes. Please make your way to the main entrance, and thank you for visiting.' Add the delivery style 'calm, warm, and unhurried.'
4. Select Generate audio, then play the result under Listen. Check whether the words are complete, the pacing matches the direction, and the voice remains clear. Do not use voice replication for this test.
5. Switch to Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS, generate the identical script once, and compare clarity and delivery. Stop after the comparison, review current [API pricing and data-use terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing), and revoke the key in Google AI Studio if you do not plan to keep using it.

</PracticalGuide>
