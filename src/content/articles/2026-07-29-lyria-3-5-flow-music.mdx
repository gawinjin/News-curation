## TL;DR

Google is rolling out Lyria 3.5 in Flow Music with more natural melodies, clearer and more expressive vocals, stronger prompt following, and direct control over tempo and duration. A two-song comparison can quickly show whether changing one style phrase produces a useful musical difference.

## What's New

Lyria 3.5 is Google's newest music-generation model, and it is rolling out through Flow Music. Google says it produces richer, more natural-sounding melodies while following prompts more closely.

The model is also designed to understand song structure when writing lyrics. Google reports more realistic and expressive vocals, clearer pronunciation, and more direct control over a track's tempo and length.

## Why It Matters

Music generation is easier to judge when a small prompt change leads to an audible difference. Better prompt following and clearer vocals could make it simpler to compare ideas without changing several instructions at once.

Flow Music gives eligible adults in supported regions a way to test that control directly. Keep the first experiment private, use original text, and compare two generations before deciding whether the model fits your project.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A Google Account, verified age 18+, a supported region, and credits for two generations","Headphones or speakers and a note for scoring","Original text only; no private or unlicensed inputs"]}>

1. Sign in at flowmusic.app. Stop if Google says your age or region is unsupported.
2. Select New session. Enter: Make a short, cheerful synth-pop song with original lyrics about watering a windowsill basil plant; clear vocals, a simple chorus, and no references to real artists or existing songs. Select Send, then play one result.
3. Score Song A from 1–5 for lyric clarity, vocal naturalness, and fit with cheerful synth-pop.
4. Start another New session with the same prompt, changing only synth-pop to acoustic folk. Generate, play, and score Song B with the same measures; compare the arrangements and scores.
5. Keep both songs private while testing. Before publishing or commercial use, confirm that your inputs are yours or licensed, check applicable copyright and platform rules, and disclose AI generation where appropriate.
