## TL;DR

OpenAI launched a Partner Network for companies that want help deploying AI systems, backed by a $150 million ecosystem investment and a goal to train 300,000 certified consultants by the end of 2026. For readers, the useful move is not memorizing partner names; it is learning how to evaluate AI vendors.

## What's New

OpenAI says the Partner Network is for firms that build, sell, and deliver AI solutions with OpenAI. The launch names large consulting, integration, technology, and data partners, and OpenAI says partners may later earn specializations in areas such as Codex, cybersecurity, and agents.

The company frames the problem as adoption, not model capability. In OpenAI's telling, the hard part is picking use cases, redesigning workflows, connecting systems, managing data and security, and getting people to use the result.

OpenAI's partner page gives organizations a path to express interest, join the portal, and move through apply, enable, and grow stages.

## Why It Matters

Enterprise AI is shifting from "which model should we buy?" to "who can make this work inside our messy organization?" That changes the buyer's job.

The risk is that a partner pitch can sound successful before anything useful exists. A good AI project still needs a specific workflow, a measurable outcome, a data boundary, and a named human owner for review.

<PracticalGuide timeToTry="15 min" prerequisites={["A browser","One AI project or workflow your team is considering"]}>

1. Open the OpenAI Partner Network page and scan the listed partner categories.
2. Write the work outcome you actually want, such as fewer support escalations or faster document review.
3. Ask whether your need is strategy, system integration, data cleanup, agent design, security review, or staff training.
4. If you talk to a vendor, ask for one similar deployment, the review checkpoints, the data-access boundary, and the measurement plan.
5. Do not approve a pilot until the success metric and human owner are written down.

</PracticalGuide>
